**Palette note.** The reference graded teal-and-orange because it was shot in
offices at night. Lebon Grace's photography is cream linen in daylight, and its
brand is `#f7f3ec` paper with `#23201c` ink. Copying that grade would fight the
product.

**Type note.** The reference sets its strikethrough straight onto its own dark,
graded footage. Ours is eight clips from six different shoots, from a near-white
sanding bench to a dim workshop — no single ink or white stays legible across
all of them, and a dark scrim to force it would kill the cream palette. Hence
the paper band.

## The device

A line appears. A rule draws itself through it. A truer line replaces it.

The correction must be **true and slightly against our own interest** — that is
what makes it read as a person rather than a brand. "Made in a factory / Made
for one child" is a slogan answering a complaint nobody made aloud. "This puzzle
is in stock / It does not exist yet" is an admission, and admissions get
believed.

All four are literally true of the shop:

| Struck | Replaced with | Why it is true |
|---|---|---|
| This puzzle is in stock. | It does not exist yet. | Nothing is held; every piece is cut to order |
| We ship it the same day. | We start cutting it. | The 2–3 working days is make time, not post time |
| The engraving costs extra. | It doesn't. | `details.personalisation` — "free of charge" |
| A machine made this. | A machine helped. | A laser cuts it; it is sanded and packed by hand |

**Timing of the device** (absolute frames, so it feels identical on every shot):
line in by 24 · rule crosses 42→57 · replacement settled by 84 · then ~2s to sit
and be read. Every statement shot is at least 150 frames.

## What changed from the original nine-shot script

- **Three beats added** (8, 9, 10): the child playing, the plain white version,
  a child colouring. The first version showed the object being made and argued
  about how — and never showed it being used.
- **Shots 2 and 3 use the same substitution as Script B** (a router cutting real
  wood; a macro of real engraving), because free stock has no
  laser-cutting-MDF footage. A generation model *can* make these — see
  [PROMPTS-playground.md](PROMPTS-playground.md).
- **The type moved to a paper band**, for the legibility reason above.

An all-stills version of this film also exists as `LaunchMaster` /
`LaunchVertical` — same four corrections, built from product photography only.

---

## What must stay consistent

Video models do not remember between generations. Repeat in **every** prompt:

> warm neutral palette, cream and oat tones, soft natural daylight, shallow
> depth of field, calm documentary feel, no text, no logos, no captions,
> no visible brands

And keep the wood consistent: **light MDF, honey-toned, matte** — not dark
walnut, not glossy, not painted. For anything with a child: **hands only, no
face in frame.**

---

## Audio

**The film is currently silent.**

- Room tone under everything: quiet workshop, faint birdsong at the domestic shots
- One warm piano motif, four notes, entering around 0:11 and resolving at 0:43
- The machine shots keep their own sound: a low hum and the faint tick of the head
- **No voice-over.** The type is the voice
