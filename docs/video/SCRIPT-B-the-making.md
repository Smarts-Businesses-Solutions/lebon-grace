**Modelled on:** the product-demo film — no slogans, no kinetic typography.
It works by showing the thing actually doing its work, in real detail, with real
numbers on screen. The persuasion is the evidence.

**Lebon Grace's equivalent:** one order, followed from the moment it arrives to
the moment it is posted — and then the thing being *used*, which is the point of
making it at all.

## The rule that makes this work

The reference is persuasive because **every number on screen is real**. It never
says "powerful" or "seamless" — it shows the work and lets you conclude.

So the type carries only facts, and only facts that are true of Lebon Grace
today. Each one below was checked against the shop, not written for the film:

| On screen | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| `3mm MDF · one sheet` | `details.material` — "3mm MDF, sanded by hand". Sheet stock is 3mm; the finished piece is 6mm, two bonded layers |
| `Engraving · free` | `details.personalisation` — "Add a name free of charge" |
| `Sanded by hand` | `details.material`, and about: "We cut, sand and finish" |
| `AED 15` | 41 of 41 publicly listed products |
| `Ages 3–6` | `details.age` — on every product |
| `Made to order in the United Arab Emirates` | homepage "Cut to order · Dubai workshop"; registered office is Sharjah |
| `Ready in 2–3 working days` | faq, cart, checkout, terms |

**If a fact stops being true, the film stops being usable. That is the correct
trade.**

Two things the type deliberately does **not** do:

1. **It never describes the room on screen.** Six of the twelve shots are stock
   footage of somebody else's workshop. Both stock licences bar deceptive use,
   so every caption is about the *product* — which is ours — and never about
   the place.
2. **It says nothing about the colour-in version.** See shots 9–10.

### 8 — 0:29.5–0:34 (4.5s) · a child doing the puzzle

> **Prompt:** A child's hands place wooden pieces into a wooden puzzle board on
> a pale table, warm honey-toned toys around it. **Hands and forearms only — no
> face in frame.** Soft daylight, shallow focus, unhurried, documentary.

**On screen:** `Ages 3–6`

**Why it is here:** the first cut of this film ran 42 seconds, showed the object
being made, and never once showed it being used. That was the flaw.

**The face rule is not stylistic.** Neither stock library guarantees a model
release, and Pixabay states outright that the user is responsible for checking
whether further consent is needed. A commercial advert built around an
identifiable child is the worst possible place to discover that gap. If this
clip is ever replaced, keep the constraint.

## What changed, and why

**Shots 3 and 4 as originally written do not exist in free stock.** The script
called for a laser tracing MDF with smoke lifting, and a name appearing letter
by letter — and called them "the film". 104 unique candidates were searched
across Pexels and Pixabay for those two slots. Everything returned was
industrial metal fabrication (four clips titled "laser cutting wood" were fibre
lasers throwing sparks), laser light shows, or — for "wood burning letters" —
bonfires.

So shot 3 uses a router cutting real wood in a real workshop, and shot 4 uses a
macro of letters our laser genuinely cut. **Neither film ever claims to show our
laser**, because no honest footage of it exists yet.

**The doorstep shot was cut.** Every free doorstep clip found carried visible
Amazon branding, or framed a person unflatteringly — both barred by the stock
licences. The free pool for that beat is essentially all branded US delivery
footage. Closing on the real product is truer and costs the film nothing.

**Three beats were added** (8, 9, 10), as above.

**"Sharjah, UAE" became "Made to order in the United Arab Emirates."**

---

## Two registers, on purpose

Stock clips play **full-bleed**. Lebon Grace's own product photographs sit on
**brand paper**, like catalogue plates.

That is a design rhythm — the world, then the thing — but it is also an honesty
device. The workshop footage is somebody else's workshop, and it never carries
a caption claiming otherwise.

---

## What must stay consistent

Repeat in every prompt, or the shots will not cut together:

> light honey-toned MDF, matte finish, warm neutral palette, soft natural
> daylight, shallow depth of field, small workshop not a factory, documentary
> feel, no text, no logos, no on-screen captions, no visible brands

And for anything with a child in it: **hands only, no face in frame.**

---

## Audio

**The film is currently silent.** It needs sound to work. Recorded, not scored —
this should sound like a room:

- the machine's low hum and the tick of the head, shots 2–3
- sanding, shot 6
- a child's hands on wooden pieces, shot 8
- paper and tape, shot 11
- **no music at all** until the final card, where one sustained warm note is
  enough

The absence of music is the point. The reference had none either — it trusted
the work to hold attention, and that trust is what made it convincing.

---

## The honest caveat

This film shows a *plausible* workshop, not yours. Six of twelve shots are
stock.

**Thirty seconds of real phone footage of your own laser cutting one real name
would replace shots 3 and 4 and beat everything here**, and cost nothing. The
same goes for one real child with one real puzzle, which would also retire the
model-release question entirely. If you can film either, do — and this script
becomes the shot list for what to film, which is a better use of it.
