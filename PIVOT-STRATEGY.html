3 · Pricing

The honest competitive picture

Mass-produced MDF puzzles on Amazon.ae and Noon sit roughly AED 20 to 60. Exact live figures could not be pulled (Noon blocks automated access), so treat that as indicative rather than verified.

The core constraint

You cannot win a price fight against a factory. A plant running 100,000 units has a unit cost of a few dirhams. You have laser time and your own hands. Matching their price means working for nothing.

What makes yours a different product

  1. Personalisation. A child's name engraved on the board. A factory cannot do this. Typically commands 2 to 3x and is the strongest lever available.
  2. Made to order in the UAE. Days, not weeks. No customs, local accountability.
  3. Design exclusivity. Your catalogue, not the SKU seventy other sellers list.
  4. Gift framing. Birthdays, Eid, new baby, nursery gifts. Gift buyers are far less price sensitive.
  5. The photography. Already editorial grade. A cheap price would contradict it and read as suspicious.

Recommended structure

TierProductsPlainPersonalised
StarterStencils, simple peg puzzlesAED 49AED 79

Personalisation is where the margin lives. The plain tier exists to be undercut by Amazon and to make the personalised option look obviously worth it.

Why not AED 5

MDF material                    AED  3 to  6
laser time and labour           AED  5 to 10
packaging                       AED  3 to  5
                                --------------
cost per unit before delivery   AED 11 to 21

At AED 5 you lose AED 6 to 16 per unit before anyone delivers anything. With delivery the loss doubles. No volume fixes a negative unit margin. More sales lose more money.

Free, prominent pickup protects margin on small orders and gives price-sensitive customers a way to say yes without discounting the product.

5 · Policies

  • Returns. Made-to-order items are not returnable unless faulty. State this on the product page before checkout, not buried in a policy.
  • Faulty or damaged. Free replacement within 7 days. This is what earns trust when returns are restricted, so make it prominent.
  • Personalised items. Never returnable except faulty. Confirm spelling on screen before payment.
  • Production time. "Made to order in 2 to 3 working days" on every product page.
  • Clearance. Different rules, clearly stated: stock items, returnable 7 days, unused.
  • 50/50 deposit. Now makes more sense than it did for dropshipping. It protects you if a customer abandons a piece you have already cut.

What the pivot makes obsolete

The CJ integration, sourcing agent, landed-cost gate and inventory-velocity intelligence all modelled a supplier you no longer use. Keep the code, stop running it.

Design direction

The storefront was built for 515 cheap items: dense grids, small cards, price-led. Wrong for 41 handmade pieces. It needs large photography, warm neutrals, story above spec, filtering by child age rather than price, a personalisation field visible before add-to-cart, and trust markers (made in UAE, 7-day replacement, real workshop photos).

9 · Open questions

  1. Personalisation. Can the laser engrave names reliably at volume? The pricing strategy rests on this.
  2. Capacity. Units per day. It caps revenue and decides whether a 2 to 3 day promise is safe.
  3. Material. Plain MDF, birch-faced ply, or both? Affects copy and price.
  4. Sizes. Needed per product for spec lines and shipping quotes.
  5. Clearance stock. What it is, how many, images to follow.
  6. Safety. UAE toy standards and age labelling need checking before selling children's products at scale.
